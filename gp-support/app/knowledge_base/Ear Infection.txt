Preventing and Treating Ear Infections

Is your child’s ear hurting? It could be an ear infection[cite: 1]. Children are more likely than adults to get ear infections[cite: 1]. Talk to your child’s doctor about the best treatment[cite: 1]. 
Some ear infections, such as middle ear infections, need antibiotic treatment, but many can get better on their own without antibiotics[cite: 1].

What is an ear infection?
There are different types of ear infections[cite: 1]. Middle ear infection (acute otitis media) is an infection in the middle ear[cite: 1]. 
Another condition that affects the middle ear is called otitis media with effusion[cite: 1]. It occurs when fluid builds up in the middle ear without being infected and without causing fever, ear pain, or pus buildup in the middle ear[cite: 1].
When the outer ear canal is infected, the condition is called “swimmer’s ear,” which is different from a middle ear infection[cite: 1]. 

Causes 
Middle ear infections can be caused by: 
- Bacteria, like Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae (nontypeable)—the two most common bacterial causes[cite: 1]
- Viruses, like those that cause colds or flu[cite: 1]

Symptoms
Common symptoms of middle ear infection in children can include:
- Ear pain[cite: 1]
- Fever[cite: 1]
- Fussiness or irritability[cite: 1]
- Rubbing or tugging at an ear[cite: 1]
- Difficulty sleeping[cite: 1]

When to Seek Medical Care
See a doctor if your child has:
- A fever of 102.2°F (39°C) or higher[cite: 1]
- Pus, discharge, or fluid coming from the ear[cite: 1]
- Worsening symptoms[cite: 1]
- Symptoms of a middle ear infection that last for more than 2–3 days[cite: 1]
- Hearing loss[cite: 1]

Pediatric Warning:
See a doctor right away if your child is younger than 3 months old and has a fever greater than 100.4 °F (38 °C)[cite: 1]. 

Treatment
A doctor will determine what type of illness your child has by asking about symptoms and doing a physical examination[cite: 2]. Your doctor can make the diagnosis of a middle ear infection by looking inside your child’s ear to examine the eardrum and see if there is pus in the middle ear[cite: 2]. 
Antibiotics are often not needed for middle ear infections because the body’s immune system can fight off the infection on its own[cite: 2]. But sometimes antibiotics, such as amoxicillin, are needed to treat infants, severe cases, or cases that last longer than 2–3 days[cite: 2].
For mild cases of middle ear infection, your doctor might recommend watchful waiting or delayed antibiotic prescribing[cite: 2]. 

Watchful waiting: Your child’s doctor may suggest watching and waiting to see if your child needs antibiotics[cite: 2]. This gives the immune system time to fight off the infection[cite: 2]. If your child doesn’t feel better after 2–3 days of rest, extra fluids, and pain relievers, the doctor will write a prescription for an antibiotic[cite: 2].

Delayed prescribing: Your child’s doctor may give an antibiotic prescription but will suggest that you wait 2–3 days to see if your child is still sick before filling it[cite: 2]. 

How to Feel Better 
Some ways to feel better—whether or not antibiotics are needed for an ear infection: 
- Rest[cite: 2].
- Drink extra water or other fluids[cite: 2].
- Take acetaminophen or ibuprofen to relieve pain or fever[cite: 2]. Ask your doctor or pharmacist what medications are safe for your child to take and what dose to give your child[cite: 2].

Over-the-Counter Medicine and Children
Be careful about giving over-the-counter medicines to children[cite: 2]. Not all over-the-counter medicines are recommended for children of certain ages[cite: 2]. 

Pain relievers: 
- Children younger than 6 months: only give acetaminophen[cite: 2]. 
- Children 6 months or older: it is OK to give acetaminophen or ibuprofen[cite: 2]. 
- Never give aspirin to children because it can cause Reye’s syndrome, a rare but very serious illness that harms the liver and brain[cite: 2].

Be sure to ask your doctor or pharmacist about the right dosage of over-the-counter medicines for your child’s age and size[cite: 2]. Also, tell your child’s doctor and pharmacist about all the prescription and over-the-counter medicines they are taking[cite: 2].

Prevention
You can help prevent ear infections by doing your best to stay healthy and keep others healthy[cite: 2].
- Make sure your child is up to date on vaccinations and gets a flu vaccine every year[cite: 2]. The pneumococcal vaccine protects against Streptococcus pneumoniae, a common cause of middle ear infections[cite: 2].
- Clean your hands[cite: 2]. 
- Breastfeed exclusively until your baby is 6 months old and continue to breastfeed for at least 12 months[cite: 2]. 
- Don’t smoke and avoid exposure to secondhand smoke[cite: 2].
